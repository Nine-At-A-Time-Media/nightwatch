# Stories: reporting

Roles: **reporter** (a person using the product who hits a bug; has no
GitHub access), **team member** (a developer or maintainer of the product).

- As a reporter, I can open a "report a bug" form from inside the product
  without leaving what I was doing.
- As a reporter, I can describe what went wrong in my own words, and that
  is the only thing I must provide.
- As a reporter, I can attach a screenshot of what I saw, and mark it up
  before I send it.
- As a reporter, I do not have to retype who I am when the product already
  knows me.
- As a reporter, I am given a reference for my report the moment I send
  it, and I can use it later.
- As a reporter, I can see the state of my report -- received, being
  looked at, fixed, released, or closed -- without a GitHub account.
- As a reporter, I am told when my report changes state, without having
  to go and look.
- As a reporter, I can read the replies the team chose to share with me,
  and nothing they did not choose to share.
- As a reporter, I can answer a question the team asked me, and my answer
  reaches them.
- As a reporter, I can see all of my reports in one place.
- As a reporter, I can stop being told about a report.
- As a reporter, I am told plainly when my report could not be sent, so I
  do not believe it arrived when it did not.
- As a team member, I can see the crash, the screen and the steps that sat
  behind a report, not just the words.
- As a team member, I can reply to a reporter from where I already work,
  and decide whether the reply is visible to them.
- As a team member, I can treat several reports of the same bug as one,
  and every reporter still hears the outcome.
- As a team member, I can mark a report as spam, and the person behind it
  is told nothing.
